Field guide / Review and approve

A claim and evidence checklist for AI marketing copy

AI can phrase a claim cleanly even when the evidence is weak. The review job is to separate a proven product fact from a plausible category statement, an inference, or an unsupported promise.

Practical guideFoundry editorial
Use this on a real job

Keep the current product, offer, sources, and decision owner beside the draft. The checklist works with any writing tool.

Make one row for each claim

Take the final draft and highlight every line a buyer might rely on: performance, time, savings, compatibility, safety, comparisons, guarantees, and customer outcomes. Put each claim in a row with its source, date, scope, and approved wording. A broad sentence often needs several rows because it combines separate assertions.

For example, an ingredient study can support a statement about that ingredient under the study conditions. It does not automatically prove the finished product works the same way for every customer. A customer comment can support that customer’s experience; it does not establish a typical result. Keep those scopes visible.

Use four evidence states

  • Approved product fact: supported by a current product record or verified operation.
  • Limited customer evidence: a real attributed experience with a narrow, honest scope.
  • Category or ingredient context: useful education, but not direct product proof.
  • Unknown: no reliable source yet; remove the claim or state the uncertainty.

Record who approved the final wording. Do not let an agent promote “unknown” to “approved” because a sentence sounds reasonable. Where proof is partial, narrow the sentence to the evidence you actually hold.

Review visuals as claims too

A before-and-after pair, star count, badge, chart, or product shot can make a claim without using words. Check that the image is authentic, the two sides belong together, numbers are current, and the product shown matches the item sold. If an image is illustrative, label it accurately and keep it away from the proof position.

The output of this review is a short decision: keep, narrow, source, or remove. That decision belongs in the campaign file so the next revision does not quietly restore a rejected claim.
