Field guide / Make the campaign

Match the ad promise to the landing page

An ad earns a click with a particular promise. The first screen of the destination should confirm that the visitor is in the right place and give the next useful answer.

Practical guideFoundry editorial
Use this on a real job

Keep the current product, offer, sources, and decision owner beside the draft. The checklist works with any writing tool.

Compare what the visitor sees, not what the team intended

Put the final ad beside a mobile screenshot of the actual destination. Read only the ad’s first visible promise and the page’s first screen. Does the page name the same product, buyer situation, and offer? Does it answer the question raised by the ad before introducing another angle?

Teams often match a keyword but miss the decision. An ad about choosing a shade can land on a generic benefits page; an ad about package value can land on a hero that hides the pack and price. The page may be good in isolation but still make this click work harder.

Five checks for every ad and page pair

  1. Product: the ad and page show the same item and variant.
  2. Promise: the page opens with the result or question that earned the click.
  3. Offer: price, pack, shipping, and conditions agree.
  4. Proof: the destination supports the strongest claim in the ad.
  5. Action: the next tap is obvious on a small phone screen.

Check a real mobile width, including the browser used by paid-social traffic. Desktop layouts can hide a long blank lead, awkward line break, or late offer reveal. Capture the first screen at 320, 375, and 390 pixels and inspect the tap path through the next action.

Record the mismatch as a change request

Do not write “improve consistency.” Write the exact ad line, the current page line, and the proposed replacement or new section. If changing the page would damage another campaign, use a dedicated destination for this concept. Keep the accepted offer wording locked while testing the message around it.
