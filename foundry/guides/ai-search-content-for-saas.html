How a small SaaS site can be useful in AI search

AEO and AI search do not excuse thin pages. A small software site needs crawlable, useful answers to real buyer questions and a product story that stays consistent across its pages.

Practical guideFoundry editorial
Use this on a real job

Keep the current product, offer, sources, and decision owner beside the draft. The checklist works with any writing tool.

Start with questions a buyer must answer

List the questions someone asks before trying the product: what job does it finish, what files does it need, where does the work run, what can it change, and what happens when the result is wrong? Give each substantial question a clear page with a direct answer near the top. Include a concrete workflow or checklist that is useful without buying.

For Foundry, this means guides about campaign briefs, evidence review, landing-page match, and approval boundaries. They sit close to the first pilot workflow. A generic article about every possible AI trend would add little buyer value or product authority.

Make the answer easy to crawl and verify

  • Publish the answer as HTML text with a descriptive title and one clear H1.
  • Link from the homepage and a relevant guide hub; connect related guides to each other.
  • Use a canonical URL and include only indexable pages in the XML sitemap.
  • Show the actual product state, date, sources, and limits where they matter.
  • Keep structured data consistent with visible content; do not add review or rating markup without genuine evidence.

Google’s guide for generative AI features says core SEO practices still apply, including publicly accessible, crawlable content. Its people-first content guidance asks for original, substantial value rather than pages made mainly to manipulate rankings. A sitemap helps discovery; it does not promise indexing.

Measure a narrow result

After the domain is connected, submit the sitemap in Search Console, inspect representative URLs, and monitor queries and pages. Keep a separate log of qualified planner starts from organic traffic. Review whether search visitors reach the product explanation and complete a brief. Add pages when a recurring buyer question deserves a better answer, not to reach a target page count.

Put the method to work

Foundry’s web planner can turn this into a structured campaign brief. The desktop pilot is a separate local workflow under review.
