Build a source of truth for AI-assisted marketing

A new AI session cannot be expected to know which of five documents carries the current offer. A small source-of-truth folder makes approved facts easy to find and old decisions hard to mistake for current ones.

Practical guideFoundry editorial
Use this on a real job

Keep the current product, offer, sources, and decision owner beside the draft. The checklist works with any writing tool.

Separate facts from direction

Create one current product sheet for objective facts: what is sold, what it costs, how fulfillment works, where it ships, and which guarantee applies. Keep voice and creative direction in another file. A sample headline is direction for a writer; it is not an approved claim to paste into an ad.

Store source links or filenames beside meaningful facts. If a review comes from a customer, preserve the original words, date, permission, and limits. If a product photo is a reference, note the exact variant and whether it can be used publicly.

Give every important record a status

  • Current and approved: safe to use within the written scope.
  • Draft: useful for exploration, not for customer-facing copy.
  • Rejected: keep the reason so the same error does not return.
  • Expired: a former price, promotion, shipping term, or page link.

When a new decision arrives, update the current sheet and archive the old version. Do not overwrite the evidence trail. Name who approved the change and what campaigns it affects.

Test it on a cold start

Ask someone who has never seen the project to find the current offer, two approved claims, two forbidden claims, and the destination URL. If they must search a chat or ask the owner, the source of truth is incomplete. A campaign workflow can then point to this folder rather than repeat the entire business story in every prompt.

Put the method to work

Foundry’s web planner can turn this into a structured campaign brief. The desktop pilot is a separate local workflow under review.
