Field guide / Review and approve

Make a campaign handoff a client can actually review

A client handoff should make the next decision obvious. A folder of draft ads and chat links asks the client to reconstruct the work before they can approve it.

Practical guideFoundry editorial
Use this on a real job

Keep the current product, offer, sources, and decision owner beside the draft. The checklist works with any writing tool.

Lead with the decision requested

Open the handoff with the product and offer, then state what the client is being asked to approve. Is the choice between three creative concepts, the wording of a guarantee, or the launch of one tested page? Name the decision and deadline. Put supporting research after that, where it can answer questions without hiding the request.

Each concept should fit on a comparable page or section: buyer belief, opening promise, proof required, primary copy, headline, visual direction, and destination. Consistent structure helps the client compare substance instead of formatting.

Separate ready work from open work

  • Ready for review: copy and structure the team has checked against the brief.
  • Needs client input: missing proof, offer choice, brand voice, legal or operational confirmation.
  • Production task: image, video, page, or tracking work after a concept is selected.
  • Publishing task: account changes requiring separate permission and QA.

This separation protects both sides. A client can approve the creative direction without being assumed to have approved a live budget or a claim whose evidence is missing. The team knows exactly what to make next.

Make revisions visible

After feedback, update the same handoff with a version number and a short change note. Quote the approved line exactly when a wording decision matters. Do not bury a new price, promise, or image under a generic “revisions made” note.

Before sending, ask someone outside the project to answer: what was proposed, why, what remains unproven, and what approval is requested? If they cannot do that in a few minutes, simplify the handoff. Foundry’s first workflow is built around this reviewable file rather than a status report.

Put the method to work
