Where human approval belongs in an AI marketing workflow

Approval is useful when it is attached to a concrete decision. A vague “looks good” in chat can be mistaken for permission to publish, spend, or change a customer-facing promise.

Use this on a real job

Keep the current product, offer, sources, and decision owner beside the draft. The checklist works with any writing tool.

Define the exact boundary

For a campaign workflow, there are at least three different approvals: accept a planning file, authorize production of assets, and authorize a live account change. They should not share one button or one sentence. The reviewer needs to see the exact file, version, destination, amount or account, and intended action before granting the relevant approval.

Routine preparation can proceed without an approval for every sentence. The team may gather permitted sources, draft concepts, compare claims, and prepare a page outline. Approval should concentrate at the moments where a human judgement changes the outcome or an external system.

Show enough evidence to decide

  • What will change, including the exact text or asset.
  • Where it will change, including the page or account.
  • Which proof supports the promise and which facts remain open.
  • What happens if the decision is declined or delayed.

A reviewer should be able to accept one version without accidentally accepting later edits. If the offer, target, or payload changes, the decision needs a fresh review. Record the outcome beside the campaign file so an agent does not infer approval from an old conversation.

Use the lightest safe first workflow

For a pilot, a document-first campaign pack is a sensible boundary. It can be reviewed for quality without granting Shopify or Meta access. The operator can then move the accepted material through their own production and publishing process. That makes the first result useful while the larger account-connection system is still being built and tested.
