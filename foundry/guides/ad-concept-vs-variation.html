Field guide / Make the campaign

Three ad concepts or three copy variations?

Changing the hook, the first sentence, or the background color can make two ads look different while they ask the buyer to believe the same thing. A concept changes the reason to buy.

Practical guideFoundry editorial
Use this on a real job

Keep the current product, offer, sources, and decision owner beside the draft. The checklist works with any writing tool.

Name the belief behind each idea

Write a one-line belief for every proposed ad: “This product fits my routine,” “I can choose the right option,” or “The offer is worth the price.” If two ideas have the same belief and the same proof, they are likely variations of one concept. They may still be worth testing later, but they should not fill two of three concept slots.

A true concept also changes the evidence job. A convenience angle might need a real usage sequence. A value angle might need clear package arithmetic. A confidence angle might need a fit guide or a credible demonstration. If every concept can reuse the same proof with only a new headline, look again.

Use the swap test

  1. Remove the brand name and headline from concept A.
  2. Place concept B’s headline above A’s body and proof.
  3. Ask whether the ad still makes roughly the same argument.

If it does, the concepts are too close. Repeat with the landing-page opening. A new concept should usually demand a new first answer on the page, even when the product and offer stay the same.

Plan the learning before the asset

For each concept, state what you expect to learn from a qualified test. Do not promise an outcome. The test could reveal which buyer concern draws attention, whether the proof is understood, or whether the offer needs a different explanation. Record the audience, destination, and measurement rule before changing visuals.

Once a concept earns further work, make copy and visual variations within it. That sequence keeps the account from filling with many nearly identical ads while the central buyer question remains unanswered.
