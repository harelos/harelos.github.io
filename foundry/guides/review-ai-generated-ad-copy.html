Field guide / Review and approve

How to review AI-generated ad copy before production

A polished draft is not the same as an approved ad. Review AI copy against the brief and the live offer in a fixed order so a good phrase does not distract from a wrong promise.

Practical guideFoundry editorial
Use this on a real job

Keep the current product, offer, sources, and decision owner beside the draft. The checklist works with any writing tool.

First pass: business truth

Verify the product name, variant, pack, price, discount conditions, shipping promise, guarantee, and destination. Use current source records rather than the model’s memory or a previous campaign. If one of these changes, the ad needs a new review even when the rest of the text is untouched.

Mark every meaningful claim and link it to the evidence sheet. Look for invented customer outcomes, vague authority, false urgency, and category facts written as product results. Check the creative brief and image notes as carefully as the words.

Second pass: idea quality

Read the first two visible lines at mobile width. Can a busy buyer tell what the ad is about and why to continue? Compare all concepts side by side. If they differ only in phrasing, return to strategy rather than asking for more synonyms.

Then follow the ad to the page. The page must support the same promise and show the next action without forcing the buyer to hunt for it. A strong ad with a mismatched destination is an incomplete campaign.

Third pass: production readiness

  • Read the copy aloud for awkward or translated phrasing.
  • Check line breaks and text hierarchy on a 320–390 pixel screen.
  • Confirm that no placeholder, internal note, or unsupported number remains.
  • Make sure the CTA names the actual next step.
  • Record the final approved text exactly; lock it for production.

Write a brief review verdict: accepted, accepted with specific edits, or rejected with the reason. Save that verdict beside the file. The goal is a traceable decision, not a long conversation about whether the draft “feels good.”
