Field guide / Brief the work

What belongs in an ecommerce campaign brief?

A useful brief is the smallest set of facts that lets a creator make a decision without inventing the product. For an ecommerce campaign, that means one product, one current offer, one buyer situation, and a clear file to deliver.

Practical guideFoundry editorial
Use this on a real job

Keep the current product, offer, sources, and decision owner beside the draft. The checklist works with any writing tool.

Start with the decision, then the background

Write the decision the operator must make at the end: choose a concept to produce, approve a landing-page direction, or reject an unsupported claim. A vague objective such as “make better ads” gives the team no way to finish. A brief for one test can be short, but it must name the decision owner and the review date.

Next, specify the product by its actual name and variant. Record the current price, pack size, shipping promise, return policy, and exact URL that the ad will use. If a term varies by market or customer, put the condition beside it. Do not let a creator infer the live offer from an old screenshot.

Six fields that prevent most rework

  1. Buyer situation: what the buyer is trying to solve now, and how aware they are of the product.
  2. Offer: exactly what is sold, for how much, where, and under which conditions.
  3. Approved facts: features, demonstrations, reviews, and claims with their source files.
  4. Forbidden claims: promises, comparisons, and visuals that cannot appear.
  5. Deliverable: the number of concepts, copy drafts, image briefs, and page sections expected.
  6. Approval: who can accept the file and who can authorize production or publishing.

A source field matters even when the fact looks obvious. “Customers love it” could mean a verified review, an internal hunch, or a sample from one customer. Write which. Unknowns belong in the brief as open questions, not as blank spaces for the model to complete.

A compact brief you can reuse

Product and variant: [exact item]. Current offer: [price, pack, shipping, market, URL]. Buyer moment: [specific situation]. Desired decision: [what we will choose]. Approved evidence: [source file or link per claim]. Exclusions: [claims, visuals, audiences]. Output: [three distinct concepts, copy, page outline]. Reviewer: [name]. Deadline: [date].

Before sending the brief to an AI team, read it as a new employee would. Can they tell which offer is current? Can they distinguish an approved fact from a hypothesis? Can they describe the final file without asking what “done” means? If the answer is no, fix the brief before requesting more drafts.
